Sustainable
Sports Events

Read the case study

A visual system for the DOSB’s knowledge platform on sustainable sporting events.

Client:  DOSB · via econcess GmbH
Online:  January 2025
My role:  Art Direction
Deliverable:  Design guidelines & icon concept

01 · Overview

One portal, hundreds of measures

“Nachhaltige Sport[groß]veranstaltungen” was a two-year research project by the German Olympic Sports Confederation (DOSB), the German Sport University Cologne and Öko-Institut. Its results – more than 600 measures and 268 goals – became a free online portal for event organisers, built by the agency econcess.

As Art Director at econcess, I defined the portal’s visual language and documented it in a 36-page design guideline.

The NSGV Design Guidelines – 36 pages, 9 content elements, 3 screen sizes.

02 · The design challenge

Many content types, one calm interface

The portal carries very different content – introductory questions, thematic overviews, step-by-step lists, downloads, news and detailed good-practice examples – across five thematic areas.

Editors would keep adding content long after launch. So the challenge wasn’t to design a set of pages, but to define rules that keep every future page consistent – on desktop, tablet and mobile.

03 · My contribution

Setting the direction – and writing it down

I was responsible for the visual design of the portal’s content elements, the typography, colour and button rules, and the icon concept for the entire site – all documented in the design guidelines.

Throughout the project I worked closely with the client’s stakeholders: in workshops and regular feedback rounds we discussed the designs, and I iterated them based on their input.

The guidelines became the handoff to development. The econcess development team built and implemented the portal; content, project management and the underlying research were handled by the team and the project partners.

04 · Building the visual system

A few strict rules instead of many exceptions

Typography that scales in steps.

One typeface, Segoe UI, in a clear hierarchy from Heading 1 to body text – with its own scale per breakpoint. Line heights stay at about 1.2× for headings and 1.6× for body text, so long texts read well on every screen without new rules per component.

A restrained base palette.

Three primary colours – royal blue (#003592), light blue (#86C3E4) and black – each in five opacity steps. Content elements may only use royal blue at 100% or light blue at 20%, and gradients aren’t allowed anywhere. Two rules that keep the platform calm, however much content is added.

Primary palette with opacity steps – and the rule that gradients are not allowed.

05 · Components across screens

Entry tiles: from grid to carousel

Entry tiles are the portal’s front door – short questions that lead into the main topics. On desktop they form a grid of large tiles with 60 px icons and 22 px titles, plus wide tiles for longer questions. On tablet the structure stays, in a narrower column.

On mobile the grid becomes a swipeable carousel of single tiles with 40 px icons and 18 px titles – so the first screen focuses on one question at a time.

Icon concept

Icons that show the structure

As part of the portal’s corporate design, I created the icon concept for the entire site. Each content area has its own icon colour from the same SDG-based mapping.

Every icon exists in three variants – symbol only, outlined circle and filled circle – with the line weight increased from 1 pt to 2 pt, so the icons stay readable on coloured backgrounds.

06 · Outcome

A shared reference for building and growing

The portal has been live since January 2025, in German and English.

Project recognition

The research project and the portal received the Humboldt Prize for Sustainability 2025 (1st place, Transfer) and the Sport Positive Award 2026 in the category “Inspired Innovation”.
