Memory Play

Read the case study

A memory game projected onto the table, so people living with dementia can play together – guided by a caregiver through a simple tablet app.

Context:  University project · THI
Date:  Winter 2024 / 25
Team:  2 designers

01 · Context

A global design studio

The project was part of the course “Design of Human-Machine Interfaces” at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt and of the Global Design Studio – a collaboration with universities in Australia, Canada, Sweden and Colombia on global healthcare issues. Our local partner was the Alzheimer Gesellschaft Ingolstadt e.V.

People with dementia find it hard to sequence everyday tasks. This affects their independence and often leads to social isolation and loneliness.

Research question

How can modern technology support people with dementia to live a good quality of life?

Target group

Primary users

People living with dementia – the interface has to be very simple and motivating.

Secondary users

Caregivers, relatives and trainers – their device can hold more information and options.

02 · Personas

Two people, one session

Misty, 75, a former math teacher, loves puzzles and wants to stay mentally active and included – but gets frustrated when a game is too easy or too hard. Cara, 32, a caregiver, needs activities that keep a whole group with different abilities involved.

03 · Concept

Play together, remember together

A projector turns any table into a shared memory game. Patients play by simply touching the projected cards – no device in their hands, no menus.

The caregiver prepares and guides the session on a tablet: uploads patient data, selects the players and their stage of dementia, and adjusts the projection, brightness and audio.

Scenario visuals created with DALL·E to communicate the concept.

04 · From sketch to screen

The caregiver app

We started with paper prototypes of the main flows – game categories, settings and calibration for the projection – and turned them into a clean tablet interface.
